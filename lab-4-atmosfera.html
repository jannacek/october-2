AT-LAB IV —
ATMOS_FERA

A live audiovisual performance that translates the radiative behaviour of planetary atmospheres into fog, light and spatial sound, and asks whether such an environment can work as an instrument of knowing.

Performed 24 Oct 2025 ISEA2026 paper: accepted Presentation 10–15 Nov 2026 Fulldome v.2: in development Interactive v.3: concept
ISO Amsterdam · ADE Arts & Culture Seven acts · 45–55 min NASA Planetary Spectrum Generator Music, sound design and live performance: Agustín Martínez Caram
Instead of asking what a planet looks like, ATMOS_FERA asks how an atmosphere acts, and how perception reorganizes itself in response.

Why this is an AT-LAB

ATMOS_FERA is not only a dissemination output. It is read as an inquiry with the same five movements as the other labs, with one difference that matters: the thing being read is the translation, not an audience. It was designed in 2025, before the format was named, so this is a reading in retrospect and not a design brief it followed.

  1. Design
    A seven-act sequence and a translational pipeline
    Radiative-transfer data is treated as behavioural constraint on light, sound and spatial density, not as a source of images.
  2. Intervention
    The live performance, ISO Amsterdam, 24 October 2025
    An audience that stands, moves and lingers; no fixed point of view.
  3. Reading
    Internal coherence of the mapping
    Does each act apply the same translation rules, and does the sequence expose perception to distinct regimes? This is read from the work and its documentation, not from audience responses.
  4. Reflection
    The ISEA2026 paper, peer reviewed
    States the method, the dramaturgical argument and its own limits.
  5. Feed-forward
    Fulldome v.2 and an interactive v.3
    Same method, new configurations; the second asks the body to become part of the instrument. Not yet built
No audience data, and no audience claim

No audience study was carried out for ATMOS_FERA, and none is claimed. The decision to claim nothing about the audience was taken in 2026; the plan of November 2025 (E06) still included a post-show micro-survey. In the format described in the October 2026 manuscript this lab is the limit case: the clearest example of a design-and-reflection inquiry without a participant reading. What can be defended here is a method and an argument. Perceptual or learning effects on an audience cannot.

ISEA2026 paper, Discussion (“does not rest on measured audience responses”); October 2026 manuscript, §3.4.

Seven acts Corrected

Version 1 · ISO Amsterdam · ADE Arts & Culture, 24 October 2025 · 45–55 min (the paper gives 45–50) · described as in the ISEA paper

I
Takeoff
Black and white. Natural foley dissolves into mechanical ascent and the image is stretched into slit-scanned acceleration. A deliberate perceptual vacuum: Earth’s atmospheric habits are let go before any other atmosphere enters.
II
Venus
The room thickens into a warm, diffusion-saturated yellow-orange field. Dense scattering becomes harmonic saturation in the sound, pressing against the walls.
III
Mars
The thickness is withdrawn. A fragile horizon, brittle gradients, harmonically sparse sound that puts the thinness of the atmosphere in the body.
IV
Neptune
Time and space dilate. The atmosphere becomes a slow-moving chromatic organism, an auroral drift that is closer to a gravitational field of colour than to a picture.
V
Jupiter
Overstress. Instability and tremor from shader turbulence fields, and a low-frequency gravitational rumble felt as vibration rather than heard as symbol.
VI
Return to Earth
A return to monochrome and the reintroduction of rhythm. After unfamiliar physics, the reappearance of terrestrial stability is recognised less by thought than by the body: what feels “natural” turns out to have been recalibrated.
VII
Terrestrial Sunrise · algorithmic drift
It begins as a physically grounded simulation of a terrestrial dawn and slips into AI-generated sunsets where celestial bodies behave as physical atmospheres do not allow. The drift is a controlled inconsistency, an exposure of how atmospheres are now also recognised through the conventions of generative systems. It is not offered as a claim about what an audience believes.

ISEA2026 paper, “Artistic Framework: Dramaturgy as Atmospheric Reasoning”; touring dossier (July 2026).

Three decisions that shaped the work

Read the data as behaviour, not as pictures

The spectral arrays set conditions on light, sound and density; they never supply an image. The paper’s phrase is “not simulation accuracy but phenomenological plausibility”, and the cost is the loss in translation it names: vertical stratification, planetary-scale circulation and long-term variability do not survive the crossing to human scale.

Begin with a vacuum

Act I strips the room back to black and white before any other atmosphere enters, so that the audience’s own Earth habits are let go first.

End by breaking the physics on purpose

Act VII starts as a grounded dawn and drifts into generated sunsets that no atmosphere allows. It is a controlled inconsistency, and it is offered as a question about conventions of generative systems, not as a claim about what viewers believe.

ISEA2026 paper, “Artistic Framework” and Discussion. These are the paper’s own accounts of the choices, written in first person here.

Translational modelling

Pipeline, version 1

NASA PSG spectral radiance arrays (λ–I)
→ short .mov control sequences
→ KodeLife shaders: band-averaged values drive diffusion length, chromatic weighting, temporal smoothing, turbulence gain
→ Modul8 real-time modulation
→ MadMapper projection into a fog-filled volume
→ sound composed, synthesised and designed by me, performed live and spatialised on the KIKUNO system

The data is read as behaviour. Three regimes are drawn from the radiative curves: chromatic drift, scattering profile and instability signature. They do not fix the look of an act; they set the conditions under which an atmosphere can become perceptible. The paper’s own phrasing is that the aim is “not simulation accuracy but phenomenological plausibility”.

Setting, ISO Amsterdam
  • Semi-transparent HoloGauze membrane, 3 × 2 m, with volumetric fog
  • KIKUNO spatial sound system, playing music and sound design composed, synthesised and performed by me
  • Fog as a material proxy for atmospheric density, not as theatrical effect
  • Planetary Spectrum Generator (Villanueva et al.), NASA Goddard
  • No fixed point of view; the audience stands, moves and lingers
  • Curated by Lucía Fernández Santoro (ADE Arts & Culture) and Florence Parot (ISO)

How the reviewers read it

Both reports were positive. They called the proposition clearly articulated, the translational modelling from radiative physics into perceptual environments rigorous and engaging, and the epistemic-instrument argument convincing; they noted the connection to data aesthetics, immersive experience and ecologies of place. They also made two points that I take as the paper’s weak side: it is somewhat overburdened with theory and terminology, and “occasionally reads as conceptually dense rather than demonstrably evidenced”; and something is inevitably lost when radiative physics is brought down to human scale. Both match the limits stated below.

Limits the paper names

Indeterminate correspondence

Without prior scientific context, how a physical behaviour relates to an experience may stay ambiguous. That limits what embodied experience alone can claim.

Material contingency

Legibility depends on room scale, sound pressure, projection geometry and fog density. Identical data mappings can produce different conditions elsewhere, so the work asks for site-specific calibration, not procedural replication.

Immersion against critical distance

Strong sensory integration can reduce critical distance and slide into spectacle. Dramaturgical contrast and controlled disruption address this, but do not resolve it.

Loss in translation

Vertical stratification, planetary-scale circulation and long-term variability resist perceptual articulation. The method favours behavioural plausibility over completeness.

The lenses, as read in AT-LAB IV

The lenses are not measured here. They are carried by the design of the encounter, and the page says which is which.

Concept Clarity
Clarity is not explained, it is staged: whether a person can tell one atmospheric regime from another on the evidence of light, sound and fog alone. That is a design question the sequence poses. Whether any audience member did is unknown.
Perceived Agency
A designed opportunity, not an experienced capacity: freedom of movement, standing, drifting, choosing where to attend, with no fixed point of view. Nobody’s experience of it was recorded, so this is a claim about what the room permits and not about what anyone felt. The planned interactive v.3 would make the body an input.
Transferability
The most limited of the three. Here “transfer” means reuse by another artist or facilitator; no claim is made that audiences carried anything away. What can travel is the method of translational modelling. The performance itself does not, since it needs recalibration for every room. No other facilitator, venue or domain has yet taken the method up.

What AT-LAB IV supports

● Demonstrated
  • A seven-act performance was designed, built and performed at ISO Amsterdam in front of a public audience.
  • A consistent translation from radiative-transfer data to light, fog and sound was developed and documented.
  • The account of it passed double-blind peer review at ISEA2026.
◆ Designed for
  • Legibility of atmospheric regimes to an audience.
  • Immersion kept in check by dramaturgical contrast (Acts VI–VII).
  • The method taken up for other atmospheres, rooms and data.
! Open
  • Any effect on an audience’s perception, literacy or trust.
  • Whether Act VII’s drift is noticed, and how.
  • The fulldome and interactive versions.
  • Publication of the ISEA paper in the proceedings.

Atmospheric modelling constrains the work; artistic decisions shape its realisation. The portfolio does not claim that audiences learned about atmospheres.

Where the trajectory ends up

AT-LAB IV does not resolve the PD’s questions. It states them in their most compressed form: what can an environment make legible, and how would anyone know? It is also where the evidence is thinnest on the audience side, which is why the next page sets the four labs side by side and says what the whole can and cannot support.
