PD Trajectory

Four AT-LABs read as one iterative arc: Health → Teaching → Civic → ATMOS_FERA, and what 2026 added to it.

The four AT-LABs are four runs of one question in four settings: what can an artistic environment make legible about AI and data, and for whom? Each run took something from the one before. The shared format, however, was named afterwards, by looking back across the four. That is how I describe it here, and it is why every link between labs on this page carries one of two marks: Decided at the time where a dated document shows I took the decision then, and Recognised later where I saw the connection only when reading back across the four. Both can count as research; they are not the same kind of claim. The earliest written statement of the links between the labs is the iteration map of 4 November 2025, after all four had been staged.

The structure is unchanged since March: four labs, in this order. What has changed is the evidence behind each, and the care taken over what that evidence can support. See what changed since March →

The four AT-LABs

AT-LAB I · 2023 Health: sensory environments and digital sovereignty
Installation completedSecondary analysis 2026SAR25 poster presented
Context
Healthy Living as a Service (HLaS), Work Package 2. ’t Zandt, Groningen, with Hanze, Amsterdam UMC and the municipality.
Intervention
An installation that turns wearable-sensor data into sound and light, run twice: at ’t Zandt (2–4 June 2023; 52 participants on the SAR25 poster, “around fifty” attendees in the project description) and at the European Researchers’ Night, Forum Groningen (29 September 2023; 46 on the poster). The 27 forms analysed all come from ’t Zandt.
Question
Can a participatory sensory environment open people’s own relation to their data, and what does it let them say about it?
What it taught
Read in 2026: most respondents named a concrete data domain but most did not know the term “digital sovereignty”, and entanglement with data, more than control or refusal, was the common stance (15 of 27). It also suggested that “wellbeing” was too broad a frame for the question.
Where it went: SAR25 poster (Porto, May 2025) · European Researchers’ Night, Forum Groningen, 29 September 2023 · HLaS symposium, Roden, 26 October 2023 · Full page →
Recognised later A narrower question about how people name and situate AI and data · a first interest in clarity and in agency · sound as a mediumThe first iteration of the AI course ran in the same month as AT-LAB I (June 2023), so it cannot have followed from it. The nine-month report of April 2024 still carried the original title and said the route “remains at its core unadjusted”. The narrowing, and the names clarity and agency, belong to the later reading: the lenses were named in February 2026.

AT-LAB II · 2023–2026 Teaching: AI literacy as artistic practice
Four iterations runP+ARTS: proceedings pending
Context
Frank Mohr Institute, Hanze, then Minerva Faculty master’s programmes. AI: In, Within, and Around Your Practice: 1 EC (2023), 2 EC (2024), 2 EC (2025), 1 EC (2026).
Intervention
A course with conceptual framing, studio visits and a curated student showcase (2024). In 2025, a questionnaire to ten students in the first class.
Question
How do emerging artists define, use and imagine AI, and what happens when artistic practice rather than instruction is the setting?
What it taught
Definitions of AI were heterogeneous; frequent use in daily life sat alongside hesitant use in practice. Three working principles: begin with conceptual clarification; embed critical literacy; treat curation as research. From one cohort, at the outset, not as change.
Where it went: P+ARTS, Naples, October 2025 · Encounters in Artistic Research, September 2024 · ELIA Biennale and Art Ateliers · Full page →
Decided at the time Curation as a way of knowing: after the feedback meeting of 4 June 2024, a curated student showcase in September 2024. Conceptual clarification first: after the baseline questionnaire of 2025. Recognised later The limit of studying only art students, and the design of the civic labs as an answer to it.The Semiotic Protocol (June 2025) and Promptare (September 2025) came after the 2025 baseline report (May 2025), but no document of the time says they were designed in response to it.

AT-LAB III · 2025 Civic: collective meaning-making and AI
Both formats runForum+ article: accepted for review
Context
The PD Arts + Creative symposium in Tilburg (June 2025), then Placemaking Week Europe in Reggio Emilia (25 September 2025), with PublicTwin.
Intervention
The Semiotic Protocol, an image-matching game of 24 AI images and 8 prompts, then Promptare, a 75-minute hybrid workshop of prompt cards and live voting for 15–20 people.
Question
What changes when AI literacy is treated as something a group does together, rather than something an individual has?
What it suggested
Agency became plural; ambiguity could be used as material; enjoyment is not literacy. No learning outcome was measured and nobody has yet re-run the formats.
Where it went: Tilburg, June 2025 · Reggio Emilia, September 2025 · HLaS news piece · Forum+ article (April 2028 issue, if it proceeds) · Full page →
Recognised later What talk and voting cannot carry · the body and the senses as a way of reading · no audience study, on purposeThe ATMOS_FERA proposal (July 2025) and its budget (August 2025) were written before Promptare was staged (25 September 2025), so the performance did not follow from the civic lab. The link, and the decision to claim nothing about the audience, come from the 2026 reading; the minutes of 4 November 2025 still planned a post-show micro-survey.

AT-LAB IV · 2025–2026 ATMOS_FERA: atmospheric enactment as epistemic practice
Performed 24 Oct 2025ISEA2026 paper acceptedFulldome v.2 in development
Context
ISO Amsterdam, ADE Arts & Culture. My music, sound design, visuals and live performance; spatial sound system by KIKUNO. ISEA2026 (Dubai, 6–15 November 2026, academic programme online).
Intervention
A seven-act audiovisual performance, 45–55 minutes. NASA Planetary Spectrum Generator data translated into fog, light and spatial sound.
Question
Can an immersive environment work as an epistemic instrument, producing atmospheric knowledge by structured encounter rather than depiction?
What it supports
A documented translation method, and an argument that passed double-blind peer review. No audience effect is claimed; the paper itself says the method calls for site-specific calibration rather than replication.
Where it went: ADE Arts & Culture, 24 October 2025 · ISEA2026 (accepted; presentation forthcoming) · Full page →

How to read the trajectory

What the arc does support
A consistent question held across four very different settings; four enacted, documented inquiries; and a format and a set of lenses that came out of looking across them.
What it does not claim
That the lenses are validated, that the format has been used by anyone else, or that any of the four labs measured a change in what participants understand.
