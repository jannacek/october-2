Roles & Learning Goals

The five learning goals of the Professional Doctorate and the four roles they take over, each read against what the portfolio can actually show.

How this page is built

The Study Guide sets five learning goals: Doing artistic and/or design research, Acting in a professional manner, Co-creating innovations, Enabling change and Disseminating for specific contexts, collectives and individuals. The first four correspond with the four roles of researcher, professional, innovator and change agent (Andriessen et al.); dissemination is the fifth. The Final Assessment Guideline asks for a coherent reflection on each goal that refers to the evidence in the portfolio. This page is that reflection, in draft. Each panel opens with one episode in which something I did was challenged and changed, then says what the work was, shows the Study Guide’s three indicators for the goal with where each one is evidenced and how far, and states the limits of the evidence. The episodes are built from dated documents, which each one names. The Assessment Map places this page among the other required components.

The goals are not boxes to tick. In this trajectory they interlock: the Researcher’s inquiry runs through the Change Agent’s civic interventions, and the Professional’s teaching feeds the Innovator’s method. For each goal this page says what the evidence is, where to find it, and where it is thin.

Learning goal 1 · role: Researcher

Doing artistic and/or design research

One consequential episodeThe counts that moved
What I assumed
That the first full coding of the 27 AT-LAB I forms (version 1.0, June 2026) was consistent with itself: the tallies, the memo and the coding table saying the same thing.
What challenged it
A consistency check on 29 September, laying the coding table beside the tallies. I had applied two counting rules unevenly: a borderline case was left out of one code and counted in another, and generic enjoyment (“Super ervaring”) was counted as a bodily or affective response for two respondents but not for a third. The memo also named the wrong respondent among those who rate their confidence highest without knowing the term, and called the overlap between defining sovereignty and speaking of control “the strongest internal warrant”.
What I decided
To state the rules first and apply them to every code, with borderline cases reported beside the count and not in it. To withdraw the “warrant” wording and say what is true: the overlap is partly produced by coding the same answers twice.
What changed
Reflexive awareness went from 5 respondents to 4, the embodied and affective register from 7 to 5, the revisit item was recounted (24 yes, 1 no, 2 blank), and the memo’s list of confident non-recognisers was corrected. The main pattern did not move: entanglement for 15 of 27, articulated control for 4.
What I learned
That a reading can be right in outline and loose in its numbers, and that the numbers are the part a committee can check. I now fix a counting rule before I count. And I treat my own lens as the thing most likely to flatter the result, which is why the null cases (a respondent who denies any influence, the empty refusal code) stay in the record.

In the record: E15 appendix (changes from v1.0, changes in v1.1), §C and §E.2–E.3; E16 §3.2 and §3.4.

Systematic inquiry, theoretical grounding and the development of knowledge that others can use. Each AT-LAB has its own question, its own kind of evidence and a feed-forward into the next. The instruments are light and chosen for the setting: a survey (AT-LAB I), a questionnaire, curatorial reflection and group dialogue (II), an analytic autoethnography (III), a documented pipeline and dramaturgy (IV).

The strongest single piece of research evidence is the 2026 secondary analysis of AT-LAB I: 27 survey forms re-read through a frozen codebook, every closed item checked against the physical originals, borderline cases recorded and not counted, with a full audit trail. Beside it stand the methods manuscript in preparation, the peer-reviewed ISEA2026 paper, the P+ARTS contribution and the COST Action contributions.

Limits of the evidence. One coder, who is also the researcher. The methods manuscript is a draft for supervisors, and its co-authorship is still to be settled. The formal ethics statement for the secondary analysis is being completed.

Learning goal 2 · role: Professional

Acting in a professional manner

One consequential episodeData I did not hold
What I assumed
That the third lab would give me participant material to analyse, as the first had. My iteration map of November 2025 lists prompt logs and photographs as instruments and “analytics needs” as the next step.
What challenged it
The workshop ran inside a partner’s event. The participants’ feedback worksheets stayed with the partner organisation, and what I held was the design, the card set, the images and my own account as facilitator.
What I decided
Not to analyse what I did not hold, and not to ask participants for more than the activity was built to take. AT-LAB III is read as an analytic autoethnography, from the facilitator’s position, with the limits of that position declared. Where I do hold the forms, in AT-LAB I, I decided to analyse them secondarily, anonymised as P01–P27, with the Dutch kept verbatim, and to list the missing ethics statement as a gap and not to present the analysis as if consent had covered it.
What changed
The evidence for the civic lab is smaller than I had planned and more honest: the manuscript says it contributes no participant dataset. The ethics statement for the AT-LAB I analysis is the open item.
What I learned
That the ethics of a format built to travel include where the data go when it lands in someone else’s hands. A partner’s decision about what to keep decides what I am entitled to claim.

In the record: E06 (iteration map, November 2025); E16 §3.3 and §4.3; E35; E25; E27 (to add).

Domain expertise, ethical responsibility and sustained reflection on practice. The artistic practice continued throughout: 4DSOUND training (2023), the ATMOS_FERA performance at ISO Amsterdam in October 2025, and the upcoming Akerk performance in March 2027. The teaching practice is documented across four iterations and the 2025 baseline study. Training is recorded on the Training & Curriculum page, with the final EC allocation being confirmed; Sound Arguments (Orpheus Institute, Ghent, with ACPA Leiden) is the main individual strand.

Research ethics, responsibility and accountability. The module Ethics of Artistic and Design Research (5 EC) was completed in October–November 2023. A data management plan is on file, and I attended the Hanze workshop on the AVG and data management on 4 April 2024. Participant-facing formats were designed around consent and collective ownership.

Reflection is documented in the Go / No-Go (15 April 2024), in the supervision record (see Supervision), and in each lab’s feed-forward, which required looking critically at the last iteration’s limits before the next was designed.

Limits of the evidence. The Study Guide asks for a reflection on the ethics module, and on my own research ethics, responsibility and accountability, in a form I can share with the committee. That reflection is not yet on this site, and the formal ethics statement for the AT-LAB I secondary analysis is being completed. Professional networks such as ARC in the North, the V2_ AI cluster and ARIAS Artificial Worlds are self-reported on this site and have not been independently checked. One contact is documented: on 19 June 2026 I was respondent to V2_’s presentation at the PD Arts + Creative symposium, which shows the contact, not membership of the AI cluster. Both Sound Arguments years still need participation statements from the organisers.

Learning goal 3 · role: Innovator

Co-creating innovations

One consequential episodeA performance cannot answer a questionnaire
What I assumed
That ATMOS_FERA could be read like the other labs, by asking the audience afterwards. The first sketch of the iteration map, in the minutes of 4 November 2025, gives the row for ATMOS_FERA a finding (“sensory immersion supports macro-system intuition”) and plans evaluation add-ons: a post-show micro-survey and a guided discussion.
What challenged it
The work itself. It is one immersive piece of 45 to 55 minutes for a mixed public, made as an atmosphere to be inhabited, not as an encounter with a way of reading it. The items that served the other labs (concept clarity, perceived agency, transfer) presuppose that people can put the encounter into words; this one is perceptual.
What I decided
Not to retrofit a questionnaire to a work that was not built for one, and to claim nothing about the audience. ATMOS_FERA enters the format as its limit case: what it can contribute is a method, translational modelling, and a documented pipeline and dramaturgy. The ISEA2026 paper makes the same cut, resting its claim on the internal coherence of the mapping and not on measured responses.
What changed
The November 2025 “finding” is no longer claimed. AT-LAB IV contributes method and documentation, and a question: whether encounters like this could be made legible through conversation, drawing or movement.
What I learned
That moving from verbal inquiry to audiovisual performance changes what can be documented and therefore what can be claimed. An artistic decision about the form of the work was also a decision about its evidence.

In the record: E06 (ATMOS_FERA row); E19 Discussion and “Limits and Constraints”; E16 §4.4 and §6; E36.

Approaches with both specific and generic use. The principal innovation is the AT-LAB format: specific because every lab is built for its own place (a village hall, a master’s course, a placemaking conference, a performance space) and generic in that the structure, design, intervention, reading, reflection and feed-forward, recurs and can be re-situated. A second is the set of three lenses, a middle layer between local judgement and cross-site comparison. A third is EMI, an exploratory vocabulary for a particular need: artist-researchers inside transdisciplinary collaborations who have to say what they contribute. It was first presented in public on 23 April 2026, at a session of the Research Centre Art & Society with participants from three consortia.

ATMOS_FERA adds a fourth, an innovation in form: translational modelling, in which radiative-transfer data constrains light, sound and fog, and a live performance is argued as an epistemic instrument. The Promptare and Semiotic Protocol toolkits are innovations by design: they are written to be taken up.

Limits of the evidence. “Generic applicability” is designed for and not demonstrated: no other facilitator has run the format or the toolkit, which is the format’s next test rather than a requirement of the programme, and the ATMOS_FERA paper says the method needs recalibration for each room. EMI has been presented and discussed, but no application of it by another team is on record. The module Understanding Discourses & Practices of Creativity and Innovation was completed in January–February 2025; its reflection is not yet on this site.

Learning goal 4 · role: Change Agent

Enabling change

One consequential episodeWhere the change was supposed to happen
What I assumed
That the change would be made in one domain. The Go / No-Go plan of April 2024 proposed to apply what the interventions taught “into a domain of change, i.e. Healthy Living as a Service”, under a title that carried a holistic-care frame.
What challenged it
AT-LAB I itself. What the 27 forms make visible is less a gap in health-technology knowledge than a felt capacity to act on one’s data, and that question is not specific to health. After the first evaluation the research question narrowed to AI and data literacy.
What I decided
To take the question into rooms where it could be asked differently: a master’s course, a placemaking square, a stage, and in 2026 into policy, through COST WG4 and a proposal for a shared guideline on AI use in the PD itself. HLaS stays a partner.
What changed
The pathways of change are now several and mostly contributions: four course iterations, civic formats built to be taken up, written input to a Green Paper whose adoption is not established. Reciprocity inside HLaS is still open. The lifestyle outcome of the original title is not what the later labs studied.
What I learned
That at this stage change means a question carried into new rooms, and not an outcome to be claimed. The Green Paper input makes the same point about governance: a record of attendance or a disclosure box can document use without showing understanding or agency, which is why the Training page keeps attendance, reflection and recognised credit apart.

In the record: E03 (the plan and the original title); Supervision, the turns of the trajectory; E24; E16 §5.2.

Starting and sustaining learning and change at individual, institutional and field level. At the community level, AT-LAB I brought wearable-sensor data and sound into a village health setting and let people say what they thought about their own data. At the institutional level, I designed and taught an AI course four times across several master’s programmes (1, 2, 2 and 1 EC), and the 2026 course is a continuation of that line. At the civic level, Promptare and the Semiotic Protocol took AI literacy into a placemaking conference and a symposium with formats built to be portable.

At the field level, I contribute to COST Action CA23158 (WG2 and WG4): a contribution on translating situated AI literacy into policy-relevant insight (February 2026) and, in Porto (2–3 October 2026), written input to a Green Paper on AI guidelines for higher arts education. A proposal for a shared guideline on AI use in the PD itself, prepared on 9 October 2026, is the newest and most local case: a working session is planned for 5 November.

Two further contributions sit between the institution and the field. On 23 April 2026 the Research Centre Art & Society invited me to present EMI to practitioners from three consortia (Just Art, HLaS and ELSA), in a session about the position of artistic research in transdisciplinary collaboration. On 19 June 2026, at the PD Arts + Creative symposium in Rotterdam, Building Bridges: Between PD Research and Institutional Practice, I was respondent to the presentation by V2_, Lab for the Unstable Media.

Stakeholder ownership was a method throughout: students were co-inquirers rather than subjects, and the civic formats are collective meaning-making tools, not data-extraction mechanisms.

Limits of the evidence. The record shows contributions made, not changes that took hold. Whether the Green Paper adopts the input, or whether the course continues beyond my own teaching, is not established. The April session and the June symposium were discussions, not changes. The module Complex Research Environments was completed in June 2024; its reflection is not yet on this site.

Learning goal 5 · added to the four roles

Disseminating for specific contexts, collectives and individuals

One consequential episodeMaking the process visible
What I assumed
That the research would be legible to others once the work was done.
What challenged it
The Go / No-Go feedback meeting of 4 June 2024, which turned on making the research easier to follow for others and on opening the process up.
What I decided
To make the process visible online, to bring the other supervisors in by expertise (academic writing, art and technology, relevance for HLaS), and to involve students, through a curated showcase in September.
What changed
Encounters in Artistic Research (September 2024) was the showcase, and curating it became part of the evidence for AT-LAB II. The course pages and this portfolio site are the online record. Writing the criteria for what to show forced me to say what AI literacy in artistic practice might look like.
What I learned
That curation can produce knowledge and not only display it, and that a form chosen for an audience (students, the school, peers) changes what the research asks. Dissemination here became a way of finding out.

In the record: E05 (notes of the meeting); E37; E40; Research ↔ Teaching.

Dissemination is part of the research here, not a step after it. Each lab ended in a form made for the people it met: the AT-LAB I poster for the SAR25 conference, the Promptare and Semiotic Protocol formats for a placemaking conference and a symposium, ATMOS_FERA as a performance at ISO Amsterdam and ADE and then as a double-blind paper for ISEA2026, the EMI session for practitioners from three consortia, and written input to a Green Paper for higher arts education. The dissemination ledger gives every output with its true status: presented, accepted, in review or in preparation.

The Study Guide asks that results are shared open access where intellectual property allows. The SAR25 poster is recorded open access by Hanze; for the other outputs the ledger links to a public page where one exists.

Limits of the evidence. Nothing is yet in print: the ISEA2026 and P+ARTS papers are accepted but unpublished, and the Forum+ article is in review. The programme does not require a journal article; the statuses are stated so that they are not overread. For most outputs there is no record of what the audience did with them afterwards.
