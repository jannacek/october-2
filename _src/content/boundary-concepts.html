Boundary Concepts

Three sensitising lenses that came out of AT-LAB practice: Concept Clarity, Perceived Agency and Transferability. They tell the analysis where to look; they do not score what it finds.

Lenses named and applied Not validated constructs Extraction as a framework: next step

The three concepts did not guide the labs. They were named in February 2026, while I was preparing a presentation for the COST Action’s second general meeting, and were then read back into the earlier material. They are claimed as sensitising concepts: they suggest directions along which to look, and their worth is judged by whether they keep making material legible, not by validation against a criterion.

A note on names

This page keeps its earlier title, “Boundary Concepts”, because that is how the three were first framed (as boundary objects for the COST meeting). In the methods manuscript they are sensitising lenses. The term boundary object (Star and Griesemer) is kept for the AT-LAB format: plastic enough to adapt locally, robust enough to hold a common identity across sites. The first lens, once written “Conceptual Clarity”, is now Concept Clarity.

A heuristic reading order, not a developmental sequence. The first two lenses read participants’ encounters; the third reads how the inquiry itself travels between settings. The sequence is not always clean or linear.

The three lenses

Lens 01

Concept Clarity

What it asks. How do participants articulate what a system is doing, and how do they locate themselves in relation to it? It is not “correct understanding”; in the course it worked as a starting condition for critical discussion.

Where it is anchored. AT-LAB II: students’ definitions of AI were heterogeneous, and “begin with conceptual clarification” became the course’s first step. This is the lens’s empirical anchor (ten students, one cohort).

Read back and across. In AT-LAB I only five of 27 respondents produced any articulation of “digital sovereignty” (three defined it, two gestured at it); 18 said outright they did not know the term. In AT-LAB III the Semiotic Protocol approached articulation from another side, asking people to reconstruct a prompt from an image, and it made ambiguity the task, where AT-LAB II had worked to reduce it. In AT-LAB IV clarity is staged rather than explained.

Limit. Which kinds of ambiguity obstruct participation and which support interpretation is a question the trajectory raises but does not settle.

Lens 02

Perceived Agency

What it asks. The felt possibility of response: the sense that one’s actions, interpretations or refusals matter within an environment shaped by AI or data. It is not mastery or control.

Where it is anchored. AT-LAB I. The survey never used the term; the lens was named later, and the 2026 secondary analysis reads the 27 forms through it. Its earliest traces are there: unfamiliarity with the term coexisting with high confidence; the few definitions of sovereignty framed as control (“de baas zijn over je eigen data”); agency appearing in forms a rating scale cannot capture, as embodied and affective unease (five respondents) and as criticism of the research itself (six).

Read across. In AT-LAB II, as interpretive authority over what AI means for one’s practice (a possible reading, not tested). In AT-LAB III, as something collective, offered by the live voting (a designed opportunity, not agency that participants reported). In AT-LAB IV, as freedom of movement in a room with no fixed point of view (a designed opportunity, not a measured response). The next section says why the difference matters.

Limit. No returned AT-LAB I form contained a refusal. That says nothing about people who did not return a form or about what the questions did not invite. The relation between this lens and Concept Clarity is posed as a question; the overlap in the coding is partly built in, and is not independent evidence.

Lens 03

Transferability

What it asks. How do forms, questions and lenses move across contexts, as distinct from findings or outcomes moving? It is not scalability. It is the reflexive lens: the least evidenced in participant material and the most important methodologically.

Where it is anchored. At the level of design, in two places. The AT-LAB III toolkit: a card deck that structures how a prompt is composed, a group format that distributes authorship, a site-image-to-prompt relation that ties the activity to a place while leaving the place substitutable, and a physical–digital loop that closes each round. And the recurrence of the six-element AT-LAB form across four very different settings.

Read across. The lens does not carry a result from one site to the next; it carries a question that each site answers in its own terms. In AT-LAB IV the paper states a hard limit: the work needs site-specific calibration, not procedural replication.

Limit. Designed for, not demonstrated. Nobody other than me has yet run the toolkit. A documented re-run by another facilitator is the format’s first named test.

October 2026 manuscript, §3.2, §5; ATLAB_I_Secondary_Analysis v1.1; AT-LAB II baseline questionnaire (2025).

Two senses of each lens, kept apart New

Two of the lenses have carried two different meanings across the four labs. A designed opportunity and an experienced capacity need different evidence, and so do the reuse of a format and the use of what someone learned. This portfolio names which one it means each time.

Each lens is anchored in one lab’s material and was then read across the others. The reading was done retrospectively, by the researcher who developed the lenses, which cannot by itself remove the risk of circularity. The test claimed is narrower: whether a lens went on making material legible where it was not developed, and where it fitted poorly. The empty refusal code in AT-LAB I and the limit case of AT-LAB IV are the two places it fitted worst.

What the lenses are not

Not a measurement system
No scores, rankings or before-and-after comparisons. After each lab the question is not “did we achieve X?” but “what conditions for clarity, agency and transfer did this environment create, for whom, and how?” The answer is interpreted, situated and partial.
Not EMI
The lenses operate within a lab, on what participants encountered. EMI operates around it, on the artist-researcher’s epistemic contribution inside a collaboration. Different levels; they should not be conflated.

What the lenses support

● Demonstrated
  • Three lenses, each anchored in one lab’s material and defined on the page.
  • Their use on the AT-LAB I forms through a frozen coding frame, with audit trail.
  • A candid record of where they fitted badly.
◆ Designed for
  • Use by other researchers and facilitators as orienting concepts.
  • Extraction as a focused framework for contexts beyond this trajectory.
! Open
  • Any independent coding of the AT-LAB I forms (single coder so far).
  • Any use by someone other than their author.
  • Whether Concept Clarity and Perceived Agency depend on each other.
