Synthesis

What I learned from the four AT-LABs, what distinguishes the format, what the labs together support, and what stays open for further research.

Four labs, three lenses and one format are the architecture of the portfolio. What I learned from running them is shorter to say: five insights, each with the evidence it stands on and its limit, and then a statement of what the format adds to what already exists.

The question for this page is not “what has been proved?” but “what does each contribution stand on, and what would strengthen it?” The answer is uneven by design. The four environments were built for their own settings and left evidence of different kinds. Some contributions are well supported, some are supported only at the level of design, and a few are claims I name as open. Each is tagged with one of three statuses used across the portfolio.

Five insights New

These are what the material taught me, stated so that they can be checked. Each is tied to an evidence ID (see the Evidence Archive) and to its limit.

1

Knowing a term and feeling confident with technology can come apart

In AT-LAB I, 18 of 27 respondents said they did not know “digital sovereignty”. Seven of those 18 nonetheless rated their confidence with digital technology 4 or 5, four of them 5; six rated it 1 or 2. The five who could articulate the term all rated 4 or 5. A missing vocabulary did not mean a missing sense of competence, so vocabulary and felt capacity should not be read as one thing.

Evidence
E15 §A.1, §B, §E.2; read on AT-LAB I
Limit
27 self-selected forms gathered for another purpose, no demographics, one coder who is also the author of the lens. A pattern in this corpus, not a prevalence.
2

People voice entanglement much more readily than control

Fifteen of 27 respondents spoke of technology as pervasive or ambivalent (“basically everything”); four spoke of sovereignty as ownership or control. The gap is what Perceived Agency was named to hold open: what is at stake may be less a knowledge deficit than a felt capacity to act.

Evidence
E15 §C, §E.1 and §E.3
Limit
The four who spoke of control are the same respondents who defined or partly articulated the term, and both codes come from the same answers (§E.3), so the contrast is suggestive, not independent evidence. The lens was named after the data.
3

Ambiguity does different work in different rooms

In the classroom, heterogeneous definitions of AI had to be reduced first, and conceptual clarification became the course’s first step in 2025. In the Semiotic Protocol, reconstructing a prompt from an image made ambiguity the task itself. The same property obstructs one activity and drives the other.

Evidence
E18 §1; E16 §5.1; E35; read on AT-LAB II and III
Limit
Ten students in one cohort; no coded data from the civic sessions, only artefacts and my own autoethnography. Which kinds of ambiguity obstruct and which support interpretation is a question the trajectory raises and does not settle.
4

Moving from words to an audiovisual performance changes what can be claimed

In the first three labs participants’ own words carried the evidence. In ATMOS_FERA there is no participant reading, and what can be defended is a method and an argument, which passed double-blind review, but no audience effect. The decision to claim nothing about the audience came in 2026; in November 2025 a post-show micro-survey was still in the plan.

Evidence
E19 Discussion; E16 §4 Table 1 and §6; E06; read on AT-LAB IV
Limit
One performance. The paper is accepted but not yet presented. The insight is about what can be claimed, not about what audiences experienced.
5

Where agency sits shifts with the medium, and so does the kind of evidence

In AT-LAB I agency shows up as something participants said, in survey answers. In AT-LAB III it is a designed collective opportunity (the vote), and in AT-LAB IV a designed spatial one (freedom to move). The move from “my data” to “we decide” to a room without a fixed point of view is a shift in what the work offers, and only the first can be read from participants.

Evidence
E15 §E; E16 §3.2 and §5; E35, E36; the two senses are set out on Boundary concepts
Limit
No study joins individual and collective agency, and no lab measures a designed opportunity as an experienced capacity.

What distinguishes the AT-LAB New

A five-stage cycle of design, intervention, reading, reflection and feed-forward is too general to establish that anything is new. What I claim as distinctive is a combination, and I state it at the level of what this portfolio shows.

Approach, as I read itWhat it typically asksWhat the AT-LAB does differently
AI and data literacy frameworks What competencies people should have, and whether they have acquired them Asks what conditions let literacy emerge in an encounter, and reads how people articulate a system and place themselves in relation to it. It does not score a competence.
Participatory and arts-based literacy projects Whether one project, in one setting, worked for its participants Keeps a recurring form and three lenses across four very different settings, so that what travels is the question and the form, not a result. Each setting keeps the evidence it can honestly support.
Research through design and annotated portfolios What one body of practice has been doing, reflected on by its maker Reflects across four labs and then states, for each, what is demonstrated, what is only designed for, and what is open. The comparison is translational, not comparative: it holds a form and lenses constant, not instruments.

Three things follow from the combination. The unit that moves between sites is a question, a form and a lens, never a finding. The artistic environment is how a short, local, pre-reflective encounter becomes describable, not decoration. And a limit case is kept inside the same format on purpose: ATMOS_FERA shows what the format can claim when there is no participant reading.

Status of this claim

The descriptions of other approaches are my summary, not a literature review. The positioning against the literature is argued in the manuscript draft that is with the supervisors (E16 §3 and §6). Nobody but me has yet used the format, so the claim of distinctiveness rests on the argument and the four worked cases, not on a comparison that someone else has run.

Three connected layers

The layers are connected but not equally developed. The first generates the material, the second reads it, the third sits beside both.

Layer 1 · Format

The AT-LAB

A recurring structure for artistic research into AI and data literacy, recognised across four environments. The most developed layer, with a methods manuscript in preparation. Being consolidated
Layer 2 · Reading

Three lenses

Concept Clarity, Perceived Agency, Transferability. Sensitising concepts, anchored in one lab each and read across the others. They orient the analysis; they do not measure. Applied · not validated
Layer 3 · Vocabulary

EMI

A practitioner-facing vocabulary for the artist-researcher’s epistemic contribution inside a collaboration. A different level from the lenses, and kept apart from the methods manuscript. Exploratory · v0.3

Five contributions and what they stand on Recalibrated

01 · The AT-LAB as a translational inquiry format

A recurring six-element form, with lenses, that carries questions between very different sites and leaves findings with their sites. Recognised after the fact and refined in the writing.

Demonstrated across four environments, by its author. Designed for re-situation by others. Open a documented re-run by another facilitator.

02 · Literacy as a condition to stage, not only a goal to teach

The shift from “how do we teach AI literacy?” to “what conditions let it emerge in an encounter?” changes what is designed and what counts as evidence. It is a research stance, and the labs are its worked examples.

Demonstrated as a way of framing and designing four inquiries. Open whether any participant’s literacy changed: nothing in the portfolio measures that, and I do not claim it.

03 · Sensitising lenses as a middle layer between the local and the comparable

Three concepts that let situated material be described and juxtaposed without being turned into scores or into generic claims about literacy. They came out of practice, not out of theory.

Demonstrated in use, with an auditable analysis for AT-LAB I. Open independent coding, and use by anyone else; the reading so far is by one researcher, in retrospect.

04 · ATMOS_FERA as translational modelling

A method that treats radiative-transfer data as behavioural constraint on light, sound and spatial density, argued as a form of atmospheric knowing. The performance enacts the method; it does not demonstrate an audience effect.

Demonstrated as a documented method and a peer-reviewed argument (ISEA2026, double-blind). Open any perceptual or learning effect; the paper itself says the method needs site-specific calibration.

05 · Artistic research as an identifiable epistemic contribution in transdisciplinary work (EMI)

A vocabulary for saying what artistic research contributes inside a collaboration, in terms collaborators can follow.

Demonstrated as a working vocabulary derived from two embedded cases, presented in public on 23 April 2026 to practitioners from three consortia. Open use outside those cases and by anyone but me; it is not a validated instrument.

What stays open for further research Updated

The trajectory does not close the question of AI and data literacy through art. It opens it more precisely. The findings are situated and partial, and the register of the claims is chosen to match.

The points below are next tests for particular claims. They are not conditions of the final assessment, and their absence is not a general deficit. What the assessment itself requires, and what still has to be confirmed with the programme, is on the assessment map.
