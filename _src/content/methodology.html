AT-LAB Methodology

An inquiry format for artistic research into AI and data literacy as it takes shape in situated encounters, consolidated across four environments and not yet tried by anyone else.

Methods manuscript: in preparation Re-run by another facilitator: not yet

The AT-LAB is the PD’s main methodological proposal. It is not a label for four projects, and it was not specified in advance. It is a form that was recognised across four environments after they had been staged, and then refined in the writing.

The October 2026 draft for the supervisors puts it as a translational inquiry format: it does not hold instruments constant to compare outcomes across sites; it holds a recurring form and a few sensitising lenses constant so that situated insight can be re-situated and reworked in a new place. This page sets out the form, the evidence each lab can bear, and the tests the format has not yet passed.

What an AT-LAB is

An artistically designed research environment built around a local question about AI or data. Six elements recur. What is designed is an occasion for encounter, not a solution. The artistic is not decoration; it is how a short, local, largely pre-reflective encounter becomes something that can be described and discussed.

1 · Environment
A situated artistic environment built around a local question: an installation, a course, a workshop, a performance.
2 · Encounter
People meet the environment and, through it, an AI or data system in a specific setting.
3 · Engagement
Material, dialogical or perceptual: a heartbeat drives an image; a card deck slows the writing of a prompt; a room’s light is bound to atmospheric data.
4 · Appropriate evidence
Whatever each environment can honestly support: a survey, a questionnaire, curatorial reflection, an autoethnography, technical documentation. Not treated as equivalent.
5 · Analysis
Through three sensitising lenses: Concept Clarity, Perceived Agency, Transferability.
6 · Feed-forward
What one lab made visible reshapes the design of the next, where a document of the time shows it; otherwise it is a link recognised afterwards. The mechanism by which the work accumulates.

October 2026 manuscript, §3.1 and §6.

The third step used to be called “measure” or “micro-measure”. It is now reading, because in these labs nothing is measured in the strict sense: an encounter is made legible, meaning open to description and discussion, and legible does not mean measurable. Reading may be a survey re-read through a coding frame, a questionnaire, a curatorial reflection, a facilitator’s autoethnography, or, in AT-LAB IV, the internal coherence of a translation.

The feed-forward is how knowledge accumulates across labs, and it works in two ways that this site keeps apart. Some links were decisions I took at the time, and a dated document shows it. Others are connections I recognised later, when reading back across the four. Both count as research; the lab pages mark which is which.

How the format was arrived at

Consolidated, not specified in advance

The four labs were designed for their own settings. The common structure was recognised afterwards, then refined in the writing. In research through design, reflecting on what a body of practice has been doing is a recognised way of making knowledge, and this is the sense in which the format is claimed.

The lenses were named late

Concept Clarity, Perceived Agency and Transferability were named in February 2026, while preparing a presentation for the second general meeting of COST Action CA23158, first as boundary objects and then as sensitising lenses. They were then read back into the earlier material. AT-LAB I’s survey did not ask about “perceived agency”; the lens finds traces there after the fact.

A risk named openly

The lenses were developed and applied by the same researcher, in retrospect. That cannot remove the risk of circularity. The test claimed is narrower: whether a lens went on making material legible where it was not developed, and where it fitted poorly (the empty refusal code in AT-LAB I; the limit case of AT-LAB IV).

October 2026 manuscript, §3.1–3.2.

What stays constant and what changes

Constant
  • An artistic environment as the research site
  • A situated question about AI and data
  • Evidence in the form each setting can support
  • A named domain and its stakeholders
  • A reflective loop that feeds the next lab
  • The three lenses, carried between sites and allowed to fail
Domain and object of inquiry
Health data infrastructure and personal data relations (I) → an artistic tool and a concept to define (II) → an archive of cultural images and a mediator of civic imagination (III) → an environmental condition to be perceived (IV).
Participants
Village residents (’t Zandt, Groningen) → master’s students of art and design → placemaking professionals and citizens → a festival audience.
Scale, as recorded
I: the SAR25 poster counts 52 participants at ’t Zandt and 46 at Forum Groningen; the project description and the analysis count about 50 attendees at ’t Zandt; 27 survey forms analysed. II: ten students in the studied cohort; four course iterations. III: about 15–20 people, 75 minutes, plus a small-group game. IV: one live performance, 45–55 minutes, no audience data.

Translational, not comparative

The format moves the unit of transfer up a level, from the finding to the structure that made the finding possible.

Because the four environments do not yield outcomes that can be compared, the claim is not that the labs reproduce one another’s results. It is that a form and its lenses recur, and that what moves between sites is a question rather than a result. The wish of an AT-LAB I respondent to be “the boss of your own data” does not travel as a finding about ’t Zandt. It travels as a question (where does felt control lie in this environment?) that the civic workshop took up in a collective form.

What is claimed is modest: the form can be recognised across four settings and was productively re-situated by its author. Whether anyone else can re-situate it is the next test.

What the methodology supports

● Demonstrated
  • A six-element form recognised across four very different environments.
  • Evidence handled according to what each environment can support, with the weights stated.
  • One lab (I) with an auditable secondary analysis: frozen codebook, verification log, rules for borderline cases.
◆ Designed for
  • Re-situation by other facilitators, in other cities and settings.
  • The lenses as a focused framework beyond this trajectory.
! Open: the format’s next tests
  • A documented re-run of the AT-LAB III toolkit by another facilitator.
  • A second coder on a subset of the AT-LAB I forms.
  • Whether the format reaches encounters that cannot be put into words (AT-LAB IV’s open question).
