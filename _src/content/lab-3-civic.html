AT-LAB III —
Civic

Two linked formats for reading AI images and prompts together: the Semiotic Protocol (Tilburg, June 2025) and Promptare (Placemaking Week Europe, Reggio Emilia, September 2025).

Both formats run, 2025 Autoethnography completed Forum+ article: accepted for review Open toolkit: planned
Micro probe · Tilburg Macro probe · Reggio Emilia With PublicTwin Analytic autoethnography

AT-LAB III took the AI literacy question into rooms that belonged to no course and no project: a symposium table, then a public-space conference. These audiences were more varied than a classroom, but they were still self-selected, since people chose to come to a symposium or a conference. The formats are short on purpose, an hour or so each, so that someone else could run them.

The two probes work at different scales and were run in this order. The Semiotic Protocol is a small, close, game-like reading of AI-generated images. Promptare takes the same interest (how people give meaning to what a model produces) into a larger room with a partner, a mobile interface and live voting. The page keeps what was done, what was kept as evidence and what is only designed for apart, because in this lab those three are further from each other than in the others.

What this lab did not collect

No learning outcome was measured. There is no before-and-after, no coded dataset of participants’ answers and no analysis of the feedback forms. What exists is the material of the two sessions (cards, prompts, images, a screen of votes, photographs) and my own analytic autoethnography as facilitator. The claims below are sized to that.

The two probes Corrected

Micro probe · June 2025

Semiotic Protocol

A small-group, image-matching game with a role-play frame, developed for Mediated Encounters at the PD Arts + Creative symposium in Tilburg. Participants receive 24 AI-generated images, three for each of eight speculative prompts, and must decide which images belong to which prompt. Nothing is generated live. The images are deliberately open to more than one reading and the prompts deliberately loose, so that the task cannot be settled by looking harder: it has to be argued.

The aim was not agreement. It was to see how people make an image mean something by projecting, negotiating and contesting, and to find out whether that negotiation is a usable form of critical reading.

Reading method: analytic autoethnography (Anderson 2006), written from the facilitator’s position. The session was not coded.

Macro probe · September 2025

Promptare

A hybrid, roughly 75-minute workshop made with PublicTwin (Michał Kasperski and Jacek Baczkowski) for Placemaking Week Europe. The name joins “prompting” with the Italian verb for inviting. Participants worked with a physical card set whose prompts are in the manner of oblique strategies (“you’ve just arrived from another planet”, “think in centuries”), and with a mobile interface reached by QR code where images were shown and voted on, with playful “likes” as the register of judgement.

The question it carried: when a group uses AI to imagine its own city, who frames the prompt, and what happens to the images nobody votes for?

About fifteen to twenty participants. Observations and feedback forms formed part of the design; the forms stayed with the partner organisation and have not been analysed for this PD.

The Forum+ article states the limit plainly: I kept no dataset of what anyone “learned”. What it has instead is the artefacts and the facilitator’s memory of two rooms. That is why the section below speaks of what the sessions suggest, and why “enjoyment is not literacy and engagement is not empowerment” is a rule I apply to my own results.

What the sessions suggest

Interpretive readings from the facilitator’s position, held at the level the material allows.

Agency became plural

In AT-LAB I the preferred stance was individual: to be “the boss of your own data”. In the civic rooms, the live vote made judgement visible as a group decision. Several participants appeared more at ease questioning an image when they were doing it with others. This is an observation about how the formats distribute authority, not a measure of confidence.

Ambiguity worked as material

Because the images could be read more than one way, disagreement was productive rather than a failure to understand. The protocol used it as a resource. Whether that makes the disagreement better than consensus for building literacy is a design bet, not a result.

Play lowered the threshold, and may have limited the depth

Both formats were enjoyable for the people in them. That is a strength for participation. It is also the reason I do not read engagement as evidence of understanding.

The lenses, as read in AT-LAB III

Concept Clarity
Not tested as such. The formats let heterogeneous framings of AI (science-fiction, news, own use) show up and be put side by side. A shared framing, where it appeared, was temporary and made by the group in the moment, not an individual gain in definition.
Perceived Agency
The lab’s clearest contribution to the lens: agency read as collective rather than individual, a shift from AT-LAB I’s “my data” to a “we decide”. It is a designed opportunity (the vote and the card sort are ways of deciding together that the format offers), not an experienced capacity reported by participants. AT-LAB I’s traces, by contrast, are expressed agency in survey answers. Not measured; read from the design of the voting and the facilitator’s observations.
Transferability
Designed for, not demonstrated. The formats were written to be portable, and the cards and instructions can be handed on. Here “transfer” means another facilitator reusing the format; participants carrying anything into everyday life is not claimed. Nobody other than me has yet run Promptare or the Semiotic Protocol in another city. “What travels is not a result; it is a way of asking.”

What AT-LAB III supports

● Demonstrated
  • Two civic formats were designed, run in front of real groups and documented as artefacts.
  • Promptare was carried out with an outside partner at an international conference, with a public news record (HLaS).
  • The Semiotic Protocol has been read through a completed analytic autoethnography.
  • The work has entered peer review as a written account (Forum+).
◆ Designed for
  • Other facilitators running the formats in other cities and groups.
  • Ambiguity and collective voting as ways of practising critical reading of AI images.
  • An open toolkit that carries the cards and the facilitation notes.
! Open
  • Any claim about what participants learned or whether their literacy changed.
  • Analysis of the Promptare feedback forms.
  • A re-run by someone else.
  • Whether the formats work without the facilitator who designed them.
